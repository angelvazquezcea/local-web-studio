# Partnerships

For a local studio, two or three good partners are the most reliable source of clients.

## Good partner types

| Partner | What they bring | What you bring |
|---|---|---|
| Drone / photo / video | Visual material, clients who want to show their property | A place to publish their work, a combined pack |
| Accountant / gestoría | Trust of every small business owner | A service their clients ask about |
| Printer / sign maker | Clients doing branding and signage | QR codes, matching design |
| Cooperatives, associations, chambers | Member lists, events | Member discount, talks |
| Rural tourism / booking platforms | Hosts needing direct bookings | Sites that complement listings |

## Setting up a deal (write it down, even informally)

- **Who refers whom**, and how a referral is recorded (a message naming the client).
- **Reward**: % or fixed amount, or reciprocal referrals only. Paid when the client pays.
- **Bundle pack**: one combined price; who quotes, who invoices, how the money is split; what
  each delivers and by when.
- **Media rights**: partner footage used on client sites — the client decides whether it is
  published; the partner is credited if they wish; formats and resolution to hand over.
- **Communication**: one point of contact for the client.
- **Quality**: each party can decline a project.

## Joint offer page/message

Short, outcome-led: "Drone video + website for your farm/house/business: we film it, you show
it on your own site and Google, all in one price". One example of a joint project.

## Workflow with media partners

1. Agree shot list with the client and partner based on the site plan (`site-planning`):
   hero shot, 3–6 stills, 20–40 s loop for the hero, a vertical clip for social.
2. Ask for files: H.264/H.265 1080p master + stills; you produce web versions
   (`photos-and-media`).
3. Get the client's publication approval in writing before going live.
4. Credit and tag the partner in the portfolio when allowed.
