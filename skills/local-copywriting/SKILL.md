# Local copywriting

## Rules

1. **Their words first.** Mine `client.md` quotes and call notes. The owner's phrases ("we
   still bake with the wood oven my grandfather built") are better than any copywriter's.
2. **Concrete beats adjectives.** "Since 1987, 400 hectares, 3 generations" not "quality,
   tradition, excellence". Delete *passionate, unique, quality, best, leading* unless proven.
3. **No invented facts.** Unknown numbers → `[TO CONFIRM: years in business]`.
4. **Phone-length.** Paragraphs ≤ 3 lines on mobile. Headlines ≤ 8 words. Lists over prose.
5. **Answer the visitor's real questions**: what, where, when, how much (if public), how to
   contact, what happens next.
6. **Say where.** Town, area, region naturally in the hero, about, and contact (helps local SEO
   without stuffing).
7. **Calls to action as verbs**: "Call 600 000 000", "Ask on WhatsApp", "Book your stay",
   "See how to get here".
8. **Respect hard rules** from the brief (no visits, no prices, regulated claims).
9. **Tone** from `brand-discovery`; local formality conventions; inclusive, simple language.
10. **Regulated sectors**: no health claims for food/clinics, no guarantees of results,
    alcohol and tourism registry rules — see `sector-playbooks`.

## Building blocks

- **Hero**: `<What> in <where>` + one-line differentiator + CTA. Offer 3 variants.
- **Section intro**: one sentence of why it matters to the customer, then facts.
- **Service/product item**: name, 1–2 lines what it is and who it's for, key fact, CTA if
  relevant.
- **FAQ**: question in the customer's words; answer in ≤ 3 sentences; link to contact.
- **About**: who, since when, where, why — one human detail.
- **Meta title** (≤ 60 chars): `<Business> | <Main offer> in <Town>`.
- **Meta description** (≤ 155 chars): offer + place + differentiator + action.
- **Alt text**: describe what's in the photo plainly; include place/product when natural.

## Process

1. Draft all copy in a single doc (`docs/copy.md`) by section, with alternatives for the hero.
2. Mark everything to verify. 3. Read it aloud mentally as the owner: would they say it?
4. Send to the client for fact approval before or with the demo.

When the user writes in a language you know well, write natively in it — never translate from
English drafts. For other languages, see `multilingual-sites`.
