# Discovery call

The call (or visit, often better in rural areas) is where the site is really designed.
A good brief prevents 80% of later changes. Aim for 60–90 minutes; owners enjoy talking about
their business when the questions are about them, not about technology.

## Before the call

1. Read `client.md` (if any), `studio.md`, and the matching playbook in `sector-playbooks`.
2. Do a 10-minute presence audit (see `lead-prospecting` §3).
3. Prepare a question list tailored to the sector (below + playbook). Print or share it as
   short cue lines, not a questionnaire to read aloud.
4. Ask the client to have ready: logo, photos, documents (certificates, menus, price lists),
   access to existing domain/Google listing if any.

## Question map (adapt; skip what you know)

**The business**
- Name as customers say it vs legal name. Since when? Who runs it? Family history?
- What exactly do you do/sell/offer? What is the main thing? What do you *not* do?
- What makes you different from the others around? (Push for concrete facts: years,
  certifications, methods, sizes, awards, people.)
- Proudest moment / what customers always praise.

**Customers and goals**
- Who buys from you? Locals, tourists, companies, a single buyer? Where do they come from?
- How do they find you today? What do they ask most on the phone? (→ FAQ)
- What should a visitor do on the site: call, WhatsApp, book, visit, buy, ask a quote, apply
  for a job, just trust you?
- Seasonality, busy periods, things you want *fewer* of (e.g. visits, price-shopping calls).

**Hard constraints** (decide structure; write them as rules)
- Visits allowed? (Biosecurity on farms, private homes, appointments only.)
- Prices public or not? Online sales or not? Bookings via platforms (Booking, Airbnb)?
- Legal or sector rules (health claims, alcohol, animal welfare wording, tourism registry
  number displayed, professional association rules).

**Look and feel**
- Words that describe the business (ask for three). Words it must *not* feel like.
- Colours they use or love/hate; existing logo/signage/vans/packaging.
- Websites they like (any sector) and why. Competitor sites they dislike and why.

**Content and assets**
- Photos/video: own, professional, drone, none? Permission to show people/places?
- Who writes or approves texts? Languages needed (and who can check translations)?
- Reviews/testimonials they can share (real only).

**Contact and data**
- Every phone: which has WhatsApp, landlines, hours to call. Email. Address and how to
  arrive (GPS pin; rural addresses are often wrong on maps). Opening hours. Social links.
- Legal/tax data for the legal notice (legal name, tax ID, registered address).
- Existing domain, hosting, Google Business Profile, email accounts: who has the passwords?

**Project**
- Deadline or event (season start, fair, opening). Budget expectations.
- Decision maker(s). Preferred channel and times for updates.

## During the call

- Listen 70%, talk 30%. Note exact phrases the owner uses — they become the copy.
- Confirm numbers by repeating them. Mark anything uncertain `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- Explain the process, timeline, revision rounds, what is included and the care plan from
  year 2 (no surprises later). Agree the next step and date before hanging up.

## After the call — write client.md

Use `templates/client-brief.md` (or the studio's own template from `CLAUDE.md`). Fill every
field; write "unknown" rather than leaving blanks. Add a **Decisions** section with the hard
rules (e.g. "No visits — do not offer tours or a visit form"). Set status `call-done` with the
date. Then list: missing items to request from the client, and the next skill
(`proposal-writing` if not sold yet, `brand-discovery` if already accepted).

If the user pastes a transcript or rough notes, extract into the template, keep the client's
own words in quotes for later copywriting, and list contradictions to clarify.
