# Outreach messages

Local owners are busy, practical and suspicious of salespeople. Messages that work are short,
specific to their business, written like a neighbour, and ask for one small next step.

## Principles

- **One business, one message.** Mention something real: their product, town, a review, a
  photo, their missing hours on Google. Never a generic blast.
- **Lead with their benefit, not with "web".** "People searching for rural houses near X
  can't find your phone" beats "I make modern responsive websites".
- **Short.** WhatsApp ≤ 5 lines. Email ≤ 120 words. Phone opener ≤ 20 seconds.
- **One ask.** A 10-minute call, a visit, or "can I send you an example?".
- **Proof.** Link one real example site and, if possible, a name they may know.
- **Plain words.** No jargon: not "SEO", "responsive", "UX". Say "appear on Google",
  "looks good on the phone", "easy to call you".
- **Tone matches the relationship.** Family/friends: informal. Strangers: polite, warm.
  Use the local formality conventions of the language (e.g. tú/usted in Spanish).
- **Honest price signals.** If the studio publishes prices, say "from X, all included".

## Templates (adapt; never send unchanged)

**Referral intro (best):**
> Hi <name>, <referrer> gave me your number. I make websites for <sector> businesses around
> <area> — this is one I did: <link>. I saw <specific observation>. Would a 10-minute call
> this week suit you to see if it makes sense for you?

**After visiting in person:** thank them, recap the one thing you discussed, send the
example link, propose a day.

**Cold, to a published business line:** introduce yourself in one line, one observation,
one example, one ask. Offer to stop if not interested.

**Follow-up (no reply after 4–7 days):** one line, add something useful (a tip they can apply
themselves, e.g. "your Google listing shows closed on Sundays, is that right?"). Max two
follow-ups, then mark `not now` and revisit in 3–6 months.

**Phone script:** who you are → why them (observation) → the ask → if busy, ask when to call
back. Write it as bullet cues, not a speech.

## Objection handling (answer briefly, without arguing)

| Objection | Answer angle |
|---|---|
| "I have Facebook/Instagram" | Keep it; the website is what Google shows and what you own. Social links go on the site. |
| "Too expensive" | Break down what's included (domain, Google profile, first year care). Compare with one customer gained. Offer a smaller scope, not a lower price for the same scope. |
| "My nephew does it" | Great; offer help with the Google profile or a review of what they have. Stay friendly — they may come back. |
| "I don't need more customers" | Websites also bring trust, staff, suppliers, fewer repetitive calls (hours, directions). |
| "I'll think about it" | Ask what would help decide; offer to send a one-page summary; set a follow-up date. |
| "Send me info" | Send one page + one example, not a brochure. Propose a call. |

## Output

Give the message ready to copy, then one line on why it fits this lead. If the lead is in
`leads.md`, update status and next step. Never send messages yourself unless the user asks
explicitly and a messaging tool is available.
